## The inherited line and its residue read

Take a fixed admitted original route through the first rank-one arrival. On a refined progression $B=b+kP$, write its actual section after any certified bounded switches as

$$
X=p+kT+nL,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,qquad L=\ell u,
$$

where $u$ is primitive and $\ell>0$. Keep the map $x(y)=X/B+o(1)$ and the leading elapsed time $S(y)$ on the original source variable $y$. The original weight is $w(y)\,dy/I$, where $w=11!\prod_i g_i$ and $I$ is the original congruence index. The normal quotient of Task 142 identifies the real line, the exact physical normal displacement and the progression of the saturated physical line. That quotient shares the physical guard calculation. Its enlargement generally changes the available remainders. Keep $L,p,T,b,P$ for the signed calculation. [Task 136](../r2_routes/README.md) · [Task 137](../z1_stages/README.md) · [Task 142 normal quotient](../z1b_rank_one/README.md)

Let an admitted return of $p_w$ primitive updates translate by $d$. On one strict winning cell write the guard as $G=aX+a_B B+a_c\ge0$, with decrement $s=-ad>0$. Define

$$
\gamma=ap+a_B b+a_c,\qquad
\delta=aT+a_B P,\qquad
g=\gcd(s,aL),\qquad m=s/g.
$$

The remainder $j$ is uniform on the $m$ elements of $[0,s)\cap(\gamma+k\delta+g\mathbb Z)$. The first new base phase has order $e=g/\gcd(g,\delta)$, the order of $\delta$ in $\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z$. On a chosen subclass $k=k_0+e q$ and a chosen $j$, take $n_j$ with $aL n_j\equiv j-\gamma-k_0\delta\pmod s$. Take any $v\in\mathbb Z$ with $aL v\equiv -e\delta\pmod s$. The outgoing chart is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P'&=eP,\\
p'&=p+k_0T+n_j L+d\left(1+\frac{\gamma+k_0\delta+aL n_j-j}{s}\right),\\
T'&=eT+vL+\frac{e\delta+aL v}{s}d,\\
L'&=mL+\frac{aL}{g}d .
\end{aligned}
$$

The base origin is $b+k_0P$. The formulas follow by substituting $n=n_j+qv+mz$ into the exact first failed repeat $1+(G-j)/s$. Changing $n_j$ or $v$ by a multiple of $m$ changes representatives by multiples of $L'$. On a rank-zero section use $L=0$, $g=s$ and the singleton remainder. At a structural race tie, the physical exact intercept chooses the active row as in Task 136. This is the same integer congruence for tied, paired and unpaired exits. Bounded switches use their exact primitive affine maps.

The leading reward and exit mark are

$$
G_{\rm lead}=a x(y)+a_B,\qquad
\Delta=\frac{p_w G_{\rm lead}}{s},\qquad x'=x+\frac{G_{\rm lead}}{s}d.
$$

For a fixed word and winner, $j$ affects the outgoing physical sheet and the future clock. Its contribution to this block's scaled time is zero. In particular, on a homogeneous permanent line a rank-dropping return has $d=\xi u$ and $x'=0$. Its entire remaining scaled clock, from this word's section, is $p_wG_{\rm lead}/s$ on every compatible terminal remainder. The two class means can still use different return lengths, different decrements, or different probabilities of *arriving* on a sheet with such a return. This is how the rank-one terminal block of the larger cone reads the arithmetic. In that cone each final word drops rank from one to zero and all first four mean increments cancel. The clock distinguishes the distributions of terminal word and guard, even though each terminal split sums to one. ## A period for a whole paired continuation

On a homogeneous permanent line the leading normal intercept is zero, so $T=\tau u$ for an integer $\tau$. The order of the base translation on the full inherited lattice is

$$
e_{\rm ray}=\ell/\gcd(\ell,\tau).
$$

Advancing by $P e_{\rm ray}$ restores precisely the same centered coset $p+kT+L\mathbb Z$. The paired map on that coset is autonomous. Two matched centered starts therefore have identical subsequent centered difference orbits and identical difference clocks. The whole-word clocks differ from the corresponding difference clocks by at most one update each. The change of normalized position and time from the bounded base shift goes to zero, using the centered clock bound. The strict permanence inequalities hold on compact subsets of the leading region for both bases. Thus $e_{\rm ray}$ bounds the arithmetic variation of the entire rank-one paired reader on this single ingress progression, even for continuations with arbitrarily many later stages. The period of the projected reader can be smaller. This proves the indicated form of rule L on these arrivals, with the exact tangent torsion supplementing the order of the normal equation. For a nonhomogeneous or unpaired continuation, the local order $e$ and each new base coefficient must be retained.

On a paired ray write $r=R(x)>0$, with the half-ray fixed. The physical within-level comparisons, leading guard ratios and structural ties are homogeneous in the leading amplitude. The exact tie checks and weights depend on the inherited coset. The per-update division yields one reward coefficient $c_e=\Delta/r$ and one contraction $\lambda_e=R(x')/r$ for each edge $e$ on the ray. In particular, changing positive amplitude scales the duration and radius together. For a fixed compatible base refinement, the expected remaining clock on a finite truncation is $rF_A$ on ingress sheet $A$, where

$$
F_A=\sum_{e:A\to C} w_e(c_e+\lambda_e F_C).
$$

Here $w_e$ is the inherited conditional probability, and bounded short switches contribute zero scaled time. Setting $F_C=0$ at a stopped section gives the partial sum. At a stopped permanent section, initializing instead with $16/3$ gives an upper clock. If the continuation has a uniform contraction $\lambda_e\le\rho<1$ on its positive-duration arrows, these two values differ by at most $(16/3)\rho^N$ after $N$ arrows. This follows directly by multiplying the factors along an edge path and summing its probability. The same bound on each realized path gives the speed law $\nu_A$ as the limit of the finite probability mixtures satisfying

$$
\nu_A=\sum_{e:A\to C} w_e(c_e+\lambda_e\,\cdot)_*\nu_C.
$$

One can initialize stopped sections with the point mass at zero. Task 142 certifies geometric continuations on the larger saturated sheets, and the formula uses the inherited weights on the original sheets.

## Original integration and the clipped reader

For a fixed compatible arithmetic progression let $\mathcal A$ be the disjoint original records reaching their first rank-one section. Each record $A$ carries its original domain $P_A$, index $I_A$, elapsed mark $S_A$, and its exact affine leading section. For a finite or collar-truncated continuation, let $\sigma_{A,j}(y)$ be its added clock and $q_{A,j}$ its conditional probability. The contribution of all such records to one arm is

$$
\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\frac1{I_A}\int_{P_A}w(y)
\sum_j q_{A,j}h(S_A(y)+\sigma_{A,j}(y))\,dy .
$$

Subtract this expression across the two arms with the same original measure. Earlier stage 1 and 2 elapsed and section marks agree pointwise. Stages 3 through the first rank-one arrival stay in $S_A$; their weighted cancellation is verified on the cones. The bulk stage-4 estimate in Task 140 leaves this term open.

On a permanent homogeneous arrival, $\sigma_{A,j}=r_A f_{A,j}$ with $f_{A,j}$ constant in the leading amplitude on its inherited continuation. For the unclipped portion $S_A+(16/3)r_A\le1$ the tail contribution reduces to one moment for each full inherited key $a$,

$$
\sum_a F_a\sum_{A\mapsto a}\frac{1}{I_A}
\int_{P_A}r_A(y)w(y)\,dy .
$$

The prefix adds only its elapsed first moment. This pools distinct original records even if their affine source maps and densities differ. On the clipped portion use the law of $f_{A,j}$ against the original pushforward of $(S_A,r_A)$ with the test $h(S_A+r_A f)$. For a finite sequence of compatible records its integrals are exact polynomial integrals cut by $S_A+r_A f=1$. One total mean duration does not determine this clipped integral.

## A deterministic improvement of the signed remainder

## Shifts by six

Equality holds on any common domain where the class shift has a bounded original lattice match into the same centered paired orbit. Such domains include the accepted common second-exit $1/8$ collar and the union of the two closed cones. The match follows from root lattice equality modulo six and the autonomous paired map. Prior to the common second exit it uses the rising first-stage interlevel slacks and the guarded second stage. An entry for which the second exit is strictly in the permanent region cannot have a second-stage interlevel contact. More generally, a divergence between paired copies started at the same centered entrance requires an unpaired update in at least one copy before shared permanence. Both shift-by-six comparisons have unmatched original mass at most $1-\mu(H_{1/8})\le 997937411567/10^{12}$. Off this collar the width is at most $1-L$. Thus the absolute signed remainder is at most $1-\int L\,d\mu-(1-J/64)\mu(H_{1/8})$, bounded by the exact $31889640917943/32000000000000$. A separately scoped full-population comparison must account for $D^c$, whose original mass is at most $7.0078505010\%$ by Task 141. The bounds here concern $D$.
