Compare (B\equiv3\pmod{12}) minus (B\equiv5\pmod{12}) in both Task 097's model and the exact physical clock. At each anchor (b\in\{12000,48000,192000\}), compare the two pairs ((b+3,b+5)) and ((b+15,b+17)). Analyze both separately and give their equally weighted mean. Also report the difference of the two pair discrepancies and each separate class's twelve-shift. Reuse exactly 60,000 independently indexed original vectors at all twelve bases. The primary family has 18 discrepancy estimates across both readers (the two subresidue pairs and their pool at all three anchors), plus four signed comparisons of adjacent pooled discrepancies. Use independent-vector delta-method standard errors, with two-sided Bonferroni 95% simultaneous normal intervals across these 22 estimates. For a conditional mean the influence value is (I_i(V_i-\hat\mu)/\overline I), and add or subtract these values within each vector for contrasts. Use pointwise normal 95% intervals for individual-arm or supplementary contrasts and Wilson 95% intervals for each reported exclusion fraction. All intervals are Monte Carlo uncertainty estimates and do not include model approximation bias. The planned half-width is at most 1.5e−4, one third of the historical reference. The common-rank, common-lane design is what supplies precision. Use a fixed sample and report inconclusive if the planned resolution is missed.

Call the diagnostic shrinking if both readers have a resolved drop in discrepancy magnitude on at least one adjacent pooled scale, agree on its direction, and the largest-scale absolute pooled discrepancy is at most one third of the smallest-scale estimate. All primary half-widths must meet the target. Call it persistent if both readers' middle and largest-scale pooled 95% simultaneous intervals lie past 3e−4 in the same direction, both largest-scale subresidue pairs have that sign, both middle-to-largest change intervals are inside ±1.5e−4, and the planned half-widths hold. Otherwise call it inconclusive. A shrinking discrepancy can coexist with a smaller unresolved residual. A statistically resolved change in a signed clock contrast is evidence about this diagnostic, with neither a limit-law conclusion nor a determination that the two subresidues have the same law.
