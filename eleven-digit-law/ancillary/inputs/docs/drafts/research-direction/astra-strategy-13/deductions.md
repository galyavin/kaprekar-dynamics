# Deductions for the seventh compass

## 1. The restricted law and its arithmetic parameter

**Proved, conditional reduction.** Couple all bases by flooring the same eleven independent uniform digits. Let $x$ be their normalized limiting differences, with law $\mu(dx)=11!\prod g_i(x)\,dx$. Put $D=\{x\mid\tau_\Pi(x)<\infty\}$. Use the finite measure $\mathbb E[1_D f(x_B,T_B/B,Z_B)]$ for bounded Lipschitz $f$. This restriction lives on the common continuous input. Conditioning away constant finite words costs $B^{-10}$. $Z_B$ denotes the normalized differences of the first cyclic word. Theorem B′ gives $Z_B\to c=(5,4,3,2,1)/6$ in probability on the original law. If the ordered digits of the cyclic word itself are wanted, each cyclic word has a *cyclic* predecessor. Applying the borrowed digit formula to its differences, which satisfy the same all-phase bound, gives the normalized ordered word $(5,4,3,2,1,6,5,4,3,2,1)/6$ in probability. **Proved, finite path.** Fix a leading entry prefix. Its strict physical pieces are integer-affine and unimodular, with null original boundary in the limit. Fix a finite record of physical switches, repeated words and compatible arithmetic splits after it. Suppose its guards and first exits have been verified, with ties handled on the actual incoming sheet. On each resulting original chart the repeated translation is $d$, the positive decrement is $s=-a_{\rm lin}d$, and the repeat count is $1+(a-j)/s$ at $j=a\bmod s$. The possible $j$'s on $p+L$ form a coset of the image of $a_{\rm lin}L$ modulo $s$. Each has the same finite index. Any further residue condition pulls back under the affine exit to the original coset, with equally indexed preimages for classes in its image. Weighted Riemann sums on these *original* preimages give the lane law jointly with $x$ and the accumulated clock. This works with transverse $d$ and with loss of rank. A tie on an entire incoming face can have positive probability. Resolve it by its exact intercept and compatible lattice, or carry its weight as unresolved. At each finite record this proof allows a finite refinement of the base class from the pullback congruences. Its period need not divide 12. **Proved, assembly.** Consider bases going to infinity for which every fixed integer residue eventually stabilizes. Write $\beta$ for this compatible system of residues. Assume finite records as above give the chain cylinder probabilities and approaching marks along that sequence. Assume for each sufficiently small $\eta>0$ that the chain on $D$ reaches a verified first central collar in finitely many primitive switches and macroscopic stages almost surely, with the physical elapsed clock through that stop. One can instead stop at a sound terminal section. Make the collar cutoff a continuity value, or certify the boundary directly. Then the chain identifies the restricted weak limit on this sequence by the following argument.

1. Select a finite $K$ losing at most $\epsilon$ on $D$ through late leading entry, by continuity from below. Its physical cost is at most $K/B$. Preserve the original density on every finite entry branch.
2. Among countably many certified stopping records covering the remaining mass, select a finite collection losing at most another $\epsilon$. Trim accidental boundary strips if needed. Their finite-route masses and stopped clock integrals converge on this residue sequence. The uncovered physical mass on $D$ then has the corresponding limsup bound. Earlier cyclic capture outside the collar is excluded by the all-phase terminal bound on the good population.
3. The difference between the physical first-word clock and the first collar clock is in $[0,16\eta/3+o(1)]$ in scaled units. When a stage crosses the collar, use its elapsed time to the actual first hit, or certify the equivalent stopping error. Integrals of a test are therefore controlled by its Lipschitz constant times the collar width plus its oscillation times the omitted mass. Let $\epsilon,\eta$ decrease. This uses neither a uniform entry-time rate nor a uniform bound on stage types. Infinitely many stages approaching the centre are allowed.

For a fixed coarse residue $r\bmod 12$, every divergent base sequence in it admits a further sequence with all finite residues stable, by a diagonal extraction. Consequently the coarse limit exists and equals the proposed law if all these refined laws have the same projected joint measure. Conversely, if two compatible refined sequences yield different projected measures, the coarse limit cannot exist on that class. Directly establishing the same finite-record or projected limits on $r$ suffices without introducing the refined notation. This is the original-entrant application of the earlier finite-refinement passage lemma. **Proved, rank test.** With $V$ the real direction space of the incoming lattice, the exit derivative is $P=I+d a_{\rm lin}/s$. Its kernel on the ambient space is exactly $\mathbb R d$, since $a_{\rm lin}d=-s$. Finite-index residue splits preserve $V$. Thus rank drops by one exactly when $d\in V$. Otherwise it is preserved. A bound on the number of preserves controls the number of *realized macroscopic exits*. It cannot force a next such exit, bound the number of intervening short switches, or imply arrival in a terminal collar after the last exit. These are distinct from the number of positive-rank stages. ## 2. Two common exclusions for the leading basins

**Proved, extension of the paired exclusions.** Write $b=(1/6,\ldots,1/6)$ in six-gap coordinates and $h(g)=b+(g-b)/8$. Every gap of $h(g)$ is at least $7/48>1/7$, so its image is in $S$. On a strictly interlevel-paired leading step, the within-level comparisons are homogeneous about $b$. All cross-level inequalities remain strict on the segment to $b$, where the two entries within a level agree and consecutive levels are separated. Hence $K h(g)=h(Kg)$ on that step. If an original orbit converges by phases to an interior point of a wholly strictly paired periodic cell, from some time $j$ onward all its phases are strictly interlevel paired. The image under $h$ of that entire tail is an orbit in $S$, with limit the contracted point of the same paired word and ties. In particular $h$ maps its time-$j$ section into the corresponding basin in $S$. Tasks 098 and 105 give nullity there for 27 cells. The inverse homothety preserves null sets, and finite preimages under piecewise unimodular $K$ preserve null sets. Union over $j$ and cyclic phases proves global nullity for each of the 27. The centre and Two were omitted from these 27. **Proved, ray check.** At $r_*(f)$ the raw signed radius update is $(2/3+2f,1/3,-2f,-1/3,-2/3+2f)$. Its distinct magnitudes are strictly ordered $2/3+2f>2/3-2f>1/3>2f>0$ throughout $0<f<1/6$. The accepted ten-step homogeneous normal expansion applies, with a neighborhood depending on the point. A convergent tail hits the same affine ray line in finite time. Its finite preimages are null. **Proved, a general linear test for a null basin.** Take a relatively open periodic family with a fixed sorting-face itinerary of length $p$. Choose a nonzero linear form $l$ perpendicular to its phase-zero affine hull. For each phase allow every linear part $A$ of the adjacent full-dimensional branches. Pull a row from the next phase back to the preceding one by $l\mapsto lA$, retaining the phase. Suppose this generates only finitely many rows up to sign and that every row arriving at phase zero annihilates the tangent of the family. Then the basin of the relative interior has measure zero, including the union over all its possible limit points.

Indeed, fix the phase-zero witness $q_*$ and any putative limit $z$ in that relative interior. After some finite prefix, the actual branches are among the admitted ones at every phase because strict comparisons at the limit persist. Their affine maps carry the reference phase of $z$ to its next phase even when the actual point chooses a different allowed tie order. At the $n$-th subsequent return, $l(q_{j+np}-z)$ is one of finitely many signed values $v(q_j-z)=v(q_j-q_*)$. It tends to zero, hence a member of this finite set equals zero. The section lies on one of finitely many proper fixed hyperplanes. They are independent of $z$. Take finite preimages and a countable union over $j$. No individual unstable eigenvalue or estimate of typical expansion is required.

## 3. The selection test

**Proved.** Let $h$ take values in $[0,1]$. Put coupled class laws on a common space with the correct original and lane marginals. Outside a certified set of disagreement, equal *final readers* cancel pointwise. If a set $A$ contributes an integral $\Delta_A$ and the only other possibly different outcomes lie in a disjoint set $U$ with coupled mass at most $u$, then the full contrast belongs to $[\Delta_A-u,\Delta_A+u]$. The same bound holds with $\int_U |h_1-h_2|$ in place of $u$. The non-entry mass enters this inequality once on a common-source coupling if it can contribute either sign. For a raw clock, $u$ alone gives no bound on its integral there. Distinct path labels on a positive measure set can project to identical marks. Thus a small or large *sampled* frequency of differing paths settles neither a signed gap nor equality. The comparison can succeed through a signed margin before coverage of every original clock.
